Evidence ledger

List every claim. Attach the evidence. Close every row.

This deck covers one loop pattern: the evidence ledger. It shows the shape of the pattern, the loops that use it, the way it fails, and how to tell whether it fits your problem.

Where this pattern sits

Sources: Design of Agentic Systems with Case Studies, Chapter 3. Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents". The Forward Future Loop Library (85 loops).

On entering: an empty map. The set of decks covers twenty patterns, grouped by the question each family answers.

Step 1: reasoning patterns. These describe how one agent orders its own thinking and tool calls.

Step 2: workflow patterns. These describe how several model calls are wired together.

Step 3: loop patterns. These are grouped by the check that keeps a long run honest.

Step 4: this deck is the evidence ledger, the fifth loop pattern.

The claim

Claims are easy to overstate. A ledger gives every claim a row, and the run stops only when no row is open.

A loop is a prompt plus a real check plus a stop rule. In this pattern the check is the evidence attached to each claim, for and against, and the stop rule is that every row has reached an ending.

Be plain about what this is. The loops come from a public catalog of submitted ideas. Grouping them into ten patterns is a judgment made from reading them. None of it has been tested on real agent runs.

Part one

The shape

A row for every claim, evidence on both sides, one of four endings

Two slides: the four steps of the shape, then a small ledger run from five open rows to none.

Conceptual illustration

Structure

Every claim gets a row, evidence on both sides, and an ending

Source: the loop tree, pattern 5. Loops from the Forward Future Loop Library.

On entering: the problem. Claims about a product, a topic or a person are easy to overstate.

Step 1: list every claim or requirement. Nothing is checked yet. The list is the worklist.

Step 2: attach evidence to each one, for it and against it.

Step 3: settle each row. It ends supported, narrowed, flagged or unresolved.

Step 4: stop when nothing is open.

Step 5: a loop is a prompt plus a real check plus a stop rule. Here the check is the evidence on each row, and the stop rule is a count of open rows.

A ledger, run to the end

The run stops when no row is open

Source: the loop tree, pattern 5. The product and its five claims are made up. Counts are computed in code.

On entering: an empty ledger. The example is made up: a product page that makes five claims.

Step 1: list the claims. All five rows start open.

Step 2: attach evidence. Dark dots are evidence for. Red dots are evidence against. One claim has nothing either way.

Step 3: two claims have evidence for and none against. They end supported. Three rows are still open.

Step 4: the offline claim holds for reading and fails for editing. The claim is narrowed to the part the evidence supports. Loop 032 does this for product claims: fix the product or narrow the claim.

Step 5: the customer count has no evidence either way. It is flagged. It is not called false.

Step 6: the speed claim has one result for and one against. It is recorded as unresolved. Nothing is open, so the loop stops.

The loop tree names the four endings and does not define them. The one-line readings on this slide are this deck's, drawn from the loop entries.

On entering: loop 072 is for a contested claim that a decision rests on. The claim and the three sources here are made up.

Step 1: provenance. For each source the loop records where it came from.

Step 2: polarity. Each source is marked for or against, and the evidence against is counted too. Here it is two for and one against.

Step 3: negative checks. A search that came back empty is written down with its scope.

Part three

How it fails

A blank counted as disproof, or a source that does not exist

The ledger is only as good as what is attached to its rows. Both failures put something false in the evidence column.

Illustrative data

The failures

A blank is not disproof. A made-up source is not evidence.

Step 1: a row where the search found nothing for the claim and nothing against it.

Step 2: the wrong ending calls the claim false. Nothing said it was false. The right ending flags it as not verified.

Step 3: a row whose only evidence is a citation that cannot be found.

Step 4: the wrong ending marks it supported. The right ending flags it. Several loops in this pattern carry the same rule: no invented citations, data or quotes.

When to use it

Two questions, the pattern it is most easily confused with, and a checklist.

Conceptual illustration

The decision

Two questions decide whether a ledger fits

Source: the loop tree, patterns 4 and 5 and "How to use the tree". Loop 034 from the Forward Future Loop Library.

Step 1: start from the problem you have. If the risk is an overstated claim, this is the pattern.

Step 2: the pattern needs access to real sources. Without them there is nothing real to put on a row.

Step 3: the pair to tell apart. In a ledger the check is evidence on each claim. In "Maker is not the checker" the check is an independent reviewer. The loop tree notes that loop 034 fits both, and it is filed under the reviewer pattern. Its entry carries the caution that agreement is not truth.

Step 4: if no child loop fits your subject, write your own: keep the shape and supply your own check.

Conceptual illustration

Checklist

Seven checks before you run an evidence ledger

Source: the loop tree, pattern 5, and the entries for loops 032, 059, 063 and 083.

Steps 1 to 4: when the pattern fits. The first two are the pattern's problem and its need. The third comes from loop 063: define what counts as meaningful evidence first. The fourth comes from loops 032 and 083: a narrowed claim or a clear no-go is a valid outcome.

Steps 5 to 7: when it breaks. The first two are the loop tree's failure conditions. The third is loop 059's caution: one vivid quote is not a pattern.

Takeaway

Give every claim a row, with evidence for and against. Stop only when no row is open.

What would change this advice: running these loops on real work and seeing where they hold. So far this is a reading of the catalog, not a test. Next in the set: Replay until clean, where the check is a clean run from scratch.
