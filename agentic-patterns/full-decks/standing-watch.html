Standing watch

Wake, look, do one bounded thing or nothing, leave a record.

This deck covers one loop pattern: the standing watch. It shows the shape of the pattern, the seven loops that share it, the two ways it fails, and how to tell it from its neighbours.

Conceptual illustration

Where this pattern sits

Twenty patterns in three families

Sources: Design of Agentic Systems with Case Studies, Chapter 3. Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents". The Forward Future Loop Library (85 loops).

On entering: an empty map. The set of decks covers twenty patterns, grouped by the question each family answers.

Step 1: reasoning patterns. These describe how one agent orders its own thinking and tool calls.

Step 2: workflow patterns. These describe how several model calls are wired together.

Step 3: loop patterns. These are grouped by the check that keeps a long run honest.

Step 4: this deck is Standing watch, the eighth loop pattern.

The claim

A standing watch does recurring work that no one has to remember. On most wake-ups it should find nothing, say so, and stop.

The pattern is for work that recurs: errors to sweep, a changelog to keep current, a refund to chase. The loop wakes on a schedule, looks at fresh state, does one bounded thing or nothing, and leaves a record.

A caveat that holds for the whole loop family. A loop is a prompt plus a real check plus a stop rule. Grouping the 85 loops into ten patterns is a judgment from reading the catalog. None of these loops has been tested on real runs for this deck, so treat the pattern as a reading, not a result.

Part one

The shape

Wake on a schedule, look at fresh state, act once or not at all, leave a record

Two slides: the cycle, then a run of 24 wake-ups to show what a healthy watch looks like over time.

Conceptual illustration

Structure

Every wake-up ends in a record, even when there is nothing to do

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8. Loops from the Forward Future Loop Library.

On entering: the problem this pattern answers: recurring work that should happen without anyone remembering.

Step 1: the loop wakes on a schedule. Nobody starts it.

Step 2: it looks at fresh state. It does not rely on what it saw last time.

Step 3: two exits. If something changed, it does one bounded thing. If nothing changed, it does nothing. The second exit is the one the pattern needs most.

Step 4: either way it leaves a record, then sleeps until the next wake-up.

Step 5: a loop is a prompt plus a real check plus a stop rule. Reading this pattern that way: the check is the look at fresh state, and the stop rule is the clean "nothing to do" exit. That mapping is this deck's reading of the shape.

Simulation (seeded)

A run of wake-ups

Most wake-ups find nothing, and that is the loop working

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8. The run is a seeded simulation; every count is computed in code.

On entering: 24 wake-ups on a fixed schedule. At each one, the chance that something changed since the last look is 0.2. The seed is fixed, so the run is the same every time.

Step 1: the world. Something changed before 5 of the 24 wake-ups.

Step 2: the watch. It does one bounded thing at those 5 wake-ups and exits at the other 19. It leaves a record all 24 times.

Step 3: the counts. 19 of 24 wake-ups did nothing but leave a record. A watch that is quiet most of the time is doing its job. The numbers are a simulation with stated inputs, not data from a real loop.

Part two

The loops

Seven loops, four kinds of recurring work

The pattern is the parent. The seven loops are its children, each with its own subject.

Conceptual illustration

The seven loops

Seven loops stand four kinds of watch

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8. Loops from the Forward Future Loop Library.

Step 1: watch errors and changes. 004 reads production logs and fixes an actionable error, or stops if nothing is actionable. 008 reviews the day's changes each night and updates the release notes. 018 turns meaningful public product changes into a short podcast episode on a schedule.

Step 2: coordinate many repos or workers. 030 wakes every five minutes, triages repos and hands bounded work to dedicated threads. 085 classifies worker health, splits a large search into tracked chunks and reallocates by throughput.

Step 3: keep context alive for future agents. 047 builds a verified daily story of repo activity, goals and open threads.

Step 4: chase an errand to the end. 050 starts a refund claim, watches replies and deadlines, and keeps the case moving until the money arrives. That makes seven loops in four groups. Each loop is filed once, under the pattern its check belongs to.

Part three

How it fails

It keeps acting when nothing changed, or it has more authority than you meant

The loop tree names one need and two failures. The need is a clean "nothing to do" exit. The failures are acting when nothing changed, and holding more authority than you intended.

Simulation (seeded)

No clean exit

The same 24 wake-ups, with no clean exit: it acts every time

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8, "Needs" and "Fails when". Same seeded run; counts computed in code.

On entering: the same run as before. Something changed before 5 of the 24 wake-ups, and the watch with a clean exit acted 5 times.

Step 1: now a watch with no "nothing to do" exit. It acts on every wake-up.

Step 2: the counts. 24 actions, and 19 of them on a world that had not changed.

Step 3: this is the first failure the loop tree names: the loop keeps acting when nothing changed. The fix is the pattern's one stated need, a clean "nothing to do" exit.

Conceptual illustration

Authority

A heartbeat automates the looking. It does not grant authority.

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8, "Fails when", and the entries for loops 004, 030 and 050. The cautions are a reading, not the catalog's.

On entering: three of the seven loops, read for one thing: where each one stops.

Step 1: 004 reads logs, traces a root cause, fixes and verifies on its own. It ends in a pull request, not an unreviewed deploy. Its entry also warns that it needs production telemetry, which is sensitive, and that a root-cause guess can be wrong.

Step 2: 030 wakes every five minutes and hands out bounded work. It requires proof and permission before anything lands, so approvals still need a human.

Step 3: 050 acts on your behalf outside your own systems. It must stay truthful and within the permissions you granted.

Step 4: the second failure the loop tree names: the watch has more authority than you intended. A schedule makes the looking automatic. It does not decide what the loop is allowed to do.

Part four

When to use it

When the work recurs and no one should have to remember it

The loop tree's advice: pick the pattern from the problem you have, then the child closest to your subject.

Conceptual illustration

The decision

Pick the pattern from the problem, then the loop from the subject

Source: the loop tree, patterns 2, 8 and 9, and "How to use the tree". The contrast is built from the patterns' own Problem and Shape lines.

On entering: three problems that can look alike.

Step 1: recurring work that should happen without anyone remembering is a standing watch.

Step 2: a pile of small known problems is a worklist to burn down: it stops when the list is empty. Needing the agent to stop and report is an audit, gate or handoff.

Step 3: the pair to tell apart. Both look and both leave something written. A standing watch may then do one bounded thing. An audit gives a verdict and hands control back to a human. It does not act.

Step 4: if no child loop fits your subject, write your own. Keep the pattern's shape and supply your own check. The loop tree also notes that a few loops fit two patterns, and names 004 as one of them. Each loop is filed once.

Conceptual illustration

Checklist

Seven checks before you leave a watch running

Source: the loop tree, pattern 8: Problem, Shape, Needs and Fails when.

Steps 1 to 4: the pattern fits when the work recurs, each wake-up looks at fresh state, each wake-up does one bounded thing or nothing, and every wake-up leaves a record.

Steps 5 to 7: it breaks when there is no clean "nothing to do" exit, when it has more authority than you intended, or when it keeps acting though nothing changed.

Takeaway

A good watch is quiet most of the time. Give it a clean exit, and no more authority than you meant.

What would change this reading: results from real runs. This pattern comes from reading a catalog of loops, not from testing them. Next in the set: Audit, gate and handoff, the pattern for when the agent should stop and report, not keep acting.
