Perfect! Here's what probabilities([1, 2, 3]) returns:

  [0.09003057317038046, 0.24472847105479764, 0.6652409557748218]

  And here's the key insight from the comparison:

  Intermediate weights (before normalization):
  - With max subtraction: [0.135, 0.368, 1.000] (smaller, more manageable numbers)
  - Without max subtraction: [2.718, 7.389, 20.086] (larger exponentials)

  Final probabilities are identical to 15 decimal places. The False result at the end is just due to floating-point precision beyond that.

  The max subtraction is a numerical stability trick: by subtracting the maximum logit (3) before exponentiating, we get:
  - exp(1-3) = exp(-2) = 0.135
  - exp(2-3) = exp(-1) = 0.368
  - exp(3-3) = exp(0) = 1.000

  Instead of the much larger values from direct exponentiation. This prevents overflow when logits are large (imagine exp(1000) vs exp(0)), while the normalization step ensures the final probabilities are mathematically identical.
