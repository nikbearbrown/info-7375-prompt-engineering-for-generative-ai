## 2. One change to one object at a time, measured on held-out labels

**The labels.** H is 40 postings: 20 drawn from what FILTER.md v1.0 kept and 20 from what it rejected, using the committed seed. Hemanth labeled them blind, mixed in with the audit postings, and **all labels were committed (`2dffab6`, Nov 22, 3:24 PM EST) before any experiment.** No rule change, fit or prompt was tuned on H; the code refuses held-out ids when fitting (`sample.fit_threshold`; test W5).

**What 40 can show.** The set is stratified, so these numbers describe these 40 postings, not the corpus. With 40, a difference of a few postings is within noise, so every Claude change is compared with the **noise floor**: how much the identical baseline moves between two runs.

Everything below is regenerated by `python -m js3 experiment report` into `experiments/report.md`.

All 200 answers passed the output contract; there were no abstentions and no invalid answers. The raw records, with prompt hashes, times and the model each call reported, are in `experiments/E*.jsonl`.

**What moved the score, and what didn't:**
- **The noise floor was zero.** The identical baseline gave the same 40 verdicts twice. On this set, any flip can be attributed to the change, not to run-to-run variation. (That held here; JS1 recorded a flip within minutes on another posting; see section 3.)
- **The prompt moved it most, and in the wrong direction.**
  - v3 asks Claude the very question Hemanth answered, yet Claude called **all 40** "not relevant": 23 with high confidence, 17 with medium. That's 21 flips, every one from relevant to not relevant.
  - Its justifications cite the gap between one "AI Engineer Co-op" and the posting's requirements: "8+ years of experience…" (Software Engineer, Labs), "7+ years…" (Data Engineer, Product), an "Active TS/SCI clearance" (Applied AI Engineer, Public Sector).
  - Hemanth marked 19 of those 40 as worth reading. Asking the same words did not produce the same judgment: Hemanth reads "worth reading" far more generously than Claude does for an early-career candidate.
  - Agreement fell from 28 to 21, with only 40 postings and overlapping intervals. But all 21 flips went the same way, against a noise floor of zero.
- **The context and the model moved nothing (0 flips each).** Prompt v2 makes the verdict a rule: title words, skills, management words, "Location never decides". Both models applied it identically, and the rule gives the extra context nothing to act on.
  - So "context doesn't matter" is **not** what this shows. It shows that context can't matter **under a rule-bound prompt**.
- **E5 tested the context where it could matter, under the open prompt.** That's one change from E2, defined and committed before it ran.
  - The verdicts didn't move: 0 flips, still "not relevant" for all 40.
  - **The reasons and the certainty did move.** Confidence went from medium to high on 14 postings (high: 23 → 37 of 40).
  - The justifications now cite the new context: "8 months" in 33 of them, the target roles in 29, sponsorship in 13. In E2, 1 justification mentioned sponsorship.
  - Example: "matches the candidate's secondary target (Data Engineer)… But the content asks for '7+ years of experience' and the candidate has 8 months."
  - **More context made Claude surer of the same answer.** It didn't bring Claude any closer to Hemanth's judgment.
- **The filter (27/40) and the v2 judge (28/40) are nearly the same instrument.** v2 embeds JS1's rule, which overlaps the filter. With 40 postings the two can't be told apart (intervals 52%–80% and 55%–82%).
- **Not done:** changing the **tool list** (e.g. Claude with and without `search_new_postings`). No run measured it.

## 3. What persistence is not proof of

**The test:**
- JS1 saved the SHA-256 of every prompt it sent on Oct 31. `scripts/persistence_rerun.py` rebuilt those prompts with JS1's own unchanged code. **All 40 matched their recorded hashes byte for byte.**
- It then sent the 20 prompt-v2 ones again on **Nov 22 (5:43 PM EST)**, more than three weeks after **Oct 31 (2:45–5:29 PM EDT)**.

**The result:**
- **20 of 20 verdicts were the same** (10 relevant, 10 not relevant).
- Both days the call reported `claude-sonnet-5-5` (`experiments/P-rerun.jsonl`).
- Within this session, the two identical baseline runs (E0a, E0b) also agreed 40 of 40.

**None of that is evidence that anything was learned or remembered,** and the opposite wouldn't be evidence of training either:
- **Every call is a fresh conversation:** `claude -p --no-session-persistence`, an empty working directory, no tools, no settings, no MCP. Nothing from one call reaches the next, and nothing here trains or fine-tunes a model. The same bytes reaching the same served model gave the same answer. Stable output across three weeks shows a stable input and a stable model, not memory.
- **Behavior also changes across sessions without any training.** JS1 recorded the same prompt (v1) on the same posting ("Data Engineer", 5464234008) giving **relevant** at 2:44 PM and **not relevant** twice at 2:47 PM on Oct 31 (JS1 `SAMPLING-REPORT.md`). That was sampling variation, not learning. A model update by the provider would change behavior too, and that wouldn't be training on our data either.
- **Where this agent's behavior really persists is in files we can read:**
  - `FILTER.md` (v1.0 → v1.1 changed 18 decisions);
  - the prompts (`prompts/*.md`, each with a hash);
  - the labels (`audit/labels.csv`);
  - the collection state (`state/seen.json`).

  When the agent behaves differently next week, the first place to look is a diff of these files, not the model.
