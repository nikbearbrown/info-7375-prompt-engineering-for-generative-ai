## Nov 23, 2026 — Day 1: data, FILTER.md, label sample

- **What happened:** After going over the assignment requirements and the Nov 25 deadline, I started from my JS2 scraping agent. Checking the saved JS2 Greenhouse responses for Robinhood and Affirm showed that they lacked job description bodies because JS2 requested `/jobs` without `?content=true`. Since the JS3 evidence rules require quoting exact spans from the posting text, I re-collected all three boards with `?content=true` in `run-20261123-185425Z` (3 GET requests, 1.8 s, status `finished`), pulling 400 postings total: 162 from Robinhood, 218 from Affirm, and 20 from Wealthfront, all with title, location, and body text.
- **Decisions I made:** I chose to re-collect with `?content=true` rather than limiting the filter to job titles, kept Sam Rivera as a new-grad software engineering persona instead of switching to AI/ML, and dropped JS2's deliberate 404 test board.
- **What the initial survey showed:** Before any filter rules existed, scanning the 400 postings showed four patterns that shaped `FILTER.md` v1.0: 149 of 199 engineering-related titles included a seniority word; title levels did not match stated requirements (the initial survey script reported `Software Engineer II` at Affirm asking for `5+ years`); `Full Stack` appeared inside non-engineering titles like `Analytics Lead, Full Stack`; and Affirm posted separate country copies of the same role (`Remote US` / `Remote Canada`), leaving 173 postings sharing a board and title across 82 groups (91 extra copies).
- **Friction 1 — `FILTER.md` duplication during an edit:** While fixing a one-word typo in wrong version W1, a Python find-and-replace script failed to match because the title was wrapped in backticks (`find()` returned `-1`) and appended a duplicate copy of the file onto itself (230 lines, two changelogs). Claude ran that script and caught the duplicate in its own `grep` output; it restored the original 115-line single-changelog version and re-applied the fix before any commits were made.
- **Friction 2 — Redesigning the label sample:** The initial seeded sample (60 postings: 40 engineering-ish, 20 other) only had about one likely `keep` in its 30-posting held-out split, which would not have given us enough positive cases to measure filter changes. Before labeling anything, we redesigned the sampler (v2, seed `2026`) to draw 90 postings split 45/45 into `dev` and `heldout`: all 50 engineering titles without a seniority or intern word (stratum A1), 20 random seniority titles (stratum A2), and 20 random other titles (stratum B). Because stratum A1 reuses the G1/G2 keyword list, the 90-row set is a stratified benchmark rather than a uniform random sample of the 400 postings.
- **Why I accepted `FILTER.md` v1.0 as written:** I had Claude draft `FILTER.md` v1.0 from the survey summary and committed it untouched (`0041401`) before writing any filter code so the specification stayed strictly separate from implementation. I accepted the core rules because they mapped directly to concrete patterns in our data: G1 filtered out the 149/199 senior titles, R1 required full role phrases to block `Analytics Lead, Full Stack` and `Offensive Security Engineer`, G3 ran before deduplication so `Remote Canada` copies would not mask `Remote US` openings, and R2 penalized `4+ years` in the body instead of trusting title numerals. I took the `Software Engineer II` / `5+ years` example at face value without opening the raw posting myself because it fit what I expected from mid-level fintech listings, and I accepted the unmeasured weights (`0.50` threshold, `+0.30` / `-0.40` R2 points) plus the `R2 + R3 = 0.50` edge case knowing the 90-row audit would test them empirically.
- **Reference commits:** `0041401` (`FILTER.md` v1.0 alone, prior to any filter code), `b19d5ad` (collection run, loader, unlabeled label sheets, and initial logs).

Claude pointed out that the body actually asks for `1.5+ years`—identical to the other Affirm `Software Engineer II` roles I had marked `keep`—so I updated it to `keep` (`unsure: SWE II asking 1.5+ yrs, US remote`) prior to committing the label sheets.

Reviewing all `Engineer II` postings in the dataset showed that most actually ask for `1.5+ years`, with a smaller number asking for `2+ years`, `3+ years`, or no fixed minimum.
- **What I changed:** I added a `v1.1` correction note and changelog entry to `FILTER.md` while leaving the `v1.0` rules untouched so the pre-code baseline remains intact. Wrong version W2 now cites a verified `3+ years` posting (`Software Engineer II, Backend (Test Infra)`, labeled `reject`), and parsing decimal years (`1.5+` as `1.5`, not `5`) is set as the first test case for Day 2.
- **What I learned:** A quick regex used during exploratory data profiling had the exact decimal-truncation bug we need to prevent in `agent/filter.py`. Spot-checking matched sentences against the full text during labeling caught the error before it got baked into the filter logic.

## Nov 24, 2026 — Tests, filter v1.0 run, and dev comparison

- **What happened:** Claude wrote 26 tests in `tests/test_filter.py` and committed them alone (`3beb6f0`) before any filter code existed, verifying that they failed on import. Next, Claude implemented `agent/filter.py` against `FILTER.md` v1.1 (`342075a`), passing all 26 tests and confirming via `tests/wrong_versions.py` that swapping in each of W1–W5 makes its corresponding test class fail. Running the filter over all 400 postings (`evidence/filter-v1.0/`) kept 22 and rejected 378.
- **What I decided and what didn't need fixing:** A planned fix for HTML-escaped `&amp;` entities left inside scraped URLs turned out to be a non-issue—0 of the 400 URLs contain `&amp;`, while the 162 Robinhood URLs use a plain `&` (`?t=gh_src=&gh_jid=…`), which is valid—so I skipped writing a test or patch for it. I also decided not to touch `heldout` or adjust any rules until after hand-judging the v1.0 audit sheets.
- **What the dev comparison showed:** Comparing the v1.0 run against my 45 `dev` labels gave `tp = 6`, `fp = 6`, `fn = 0`, `tn = 33`. Every single one of the 6 false positives asks for `3` years of experience: because R2 assigns `0` points at `N = 3`, a role phrase (`+0.50`) plus one matching skill (`+0.10`) reaches `0.60` and clears the `0.50` threshold.
- **What was hard / what I learned:** This exposed a gap in wrong version W2. Its real-world example (`affirm:7727320003`, `Software Engineer II, Backend (Test Infra)`, asking for `3` years) is one of the 6 false positives—our W2 unit test only passed because its synthetic fixture used `5+ years`, while the real filter still let the `3`-year posting through.
- **Reference commits:** `3beb6f0` (26 failing filter tests before implementation), `342075a` (`agent/filter.py`, `tests/wrong_versions.py`, v1.0 run output, and blank audit sheets).

## Nov 24, 2026 (later) — Audit of v1.0 and rule change v1.2

- **What happened:** I hand-judged all three v1.0 audit sheets (`c87c9b6`, `cd6bfe8`): 0 of the 30 randomly sampled rejects should have been kept (a 0% sample error rate with a 95% Wilson interval of 0–11.4%, a sample estimate for this run's 378 rejects), and 11 of the 22 kept postings were genuine fits (50.0% precision across all kept postings). Among the 9 close calls (`0.40–0.60`), I initially misclicked `keep` on the lone rejected posting (`affirm:7815952003`, `Quantitative Analyst II`, score `0.40`), then wrote out the reason—that it is a capital-structuring and quantitative finance role rather than software engineering—and corrected it to `reject`, leaving 0 missed keeps in the close-call band.
- **What I decided:** Looking at the 11 false positives in `kept.csv`, 7 asked for `3` years, 3 were Robinhood backend roles asking `2+ years` with mid-level scope, and 1 was a Wealthfront iOS role. To eliminate the clear 3-year error mode without overfitting to edge cases, I had Claude update one rule in `FILTER.md` v1.2 (`6dc9c6a`) so that `N ≥ 3` years scores `−0.40`.
- **What changed and what leaked:** On `dev`, false positives dropped from `6` to `0` while keeping `tp = 6`; across all 400 postings, the exact 7 three-year keeps flipped to `reject`. However, because `kept.csv` contained all 22 kept postings from the full dataset—including rows that belonged to `heldout`—I saw some held-out postings' decisions during the audit before finalizing `v1.2`. I noted that `heldout` is therefore not strictly untouched for `v1.2` and needs to be disclosed as such in the README.
- **Reference commits:** `c87c9b6` and `cd6bfe8` (hand-judged v1.0 audit sheets), `6dc9c6a` (`v1.2`: tests seen failing first, committed together with the rule change).

## Nov 24, 2026 (later still) — The single held-out evaluation run

- **What happened:** Claude ran `agent/evaluate.py` once against the saved decisions on `heldout` (`evidence/heldout/v1.0-vs-v1.2.txt`, committed in `bdf8998`), moving false positives from `5` under v1.0 to `4` under v1.2 while holding `tp = 5` and `fn = 0` steady.
- **What I learned:** Whereas penalizing `N ≥ 3` eliminated all 6 false positives on `dev`, it only removed 1 of 5 on `heldout` because most of the held-out false positives were `2+ years` mid-level Robinhood postings or mobile-stack roles that the 3-year rule does not touch. With only 5 true keeps in `heldout` and a single-posting difference, the held-out run cannot prove `v1.2` generalizes broadly—only that penalizing `N ≥ 3` preserved 100% recall without hurting precision.
- **Reference commits:** `bdf8998` (`v1.0` vs. `v1.2` held-out evaluation).
