## 2. The six things GATE.md must say

Each heading below has the brief's question, plus facts that may help. The decisions are yours.

### Exits
The brief: every way your loop can end, and what each one reports: finished, incomplete, budget-exhausted, error. "A loop that stops is not a loop that succeeded."
- Chapter 4 ("The gate: write the exits…") lays this out as a table: *the run ends because… / status / what it reports*.
- A case to decide: some boards worked and one failed. Which exit is that?

### Budgets
The brief: the maximum boards, requests, and seconds per run, and who may change them. A budget changes only by a new written decision.
- Each Greenhouse or Ashby board takes one request for its whole job list. On 2026-11-08 each took 0.1–0.8 seconds.
- A retry is one more request.
- JS1 waited up to 60 seconds for a response before giving up.

### Repeat rule
The brief: what counts as "the same" action, and what the loop does about it.
- Chapter 4, "Repetition detectors have false positives too".
- Cases to decide: the same board listed twice; asking for the same URL again after it failed.

### Error policy
The brief: what the loop does on a failed board: stop, retry (how many times, and why a retry is sound there), or escalate to you.
- On 2026-11-08 a wrong board name got 404. A timeout or a dropped connection is a different kind of failure.

### Permission policy
The brief: which hosts the agent may call, which folders it may write, and what it may never do (apply, email, message, log in).
- Hosts: `boards-api.greenhouse.io` (Greenhouse) and `api.ashbyhq.com` (Ashby). JS1 also allowed `api.anthropic.com` and the `claude` command, for its Claude judgment.
- JS1 wrote to `runs/` (one folder per run) and `state/state.json`.
- Your boundary tests will need at least 10 cases, among them: a host off the list; a path that escapes your output folder (`..`, a look-alike folder such as `jobs-evil` next to `jobs`, a symlink); a read versus a write; and a write that needs your approval. **So the policy has to say which writes need your approval.**
- A decision only you can make: does JS2 still ask Claude about each new posting, as JS1 did?

### Wrong versions
The brief: plausible-but-wrong implementations your tests must catch.
- The brief's example: an empty board reported as "no new jobs".
- The "The gate" sections of Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 each list wrong versions for their small examples. Yours should fit this agent.

### Optional (the chapters ask for it)
Write one sentence, dated, before anything runs: what would you see if a run ended cleanly but was wrong?
