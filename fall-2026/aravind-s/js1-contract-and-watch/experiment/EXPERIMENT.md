## What this shows, and what it cannot

On the development set the revision scored 10 of 10 and the baseline 8 of 10. That gain was expected: the one change was chosen after seeing the baseline's two development misses (postings 1 and 4, both labeled as a missing specialty), so the development set is no longer a fair test of it.

On the held-out set, run once per prompt after both development runs, the scores depend on two things settled after the runs. With the contract's validator and my current labels, the baseline scored 8 of 10 and the revision 9 of 10; with my first labels it is 8 to 8; with the validator as it first ran it was 10 to 9 for the baseline (first labels) or 9 to 10 (current labels). The revision's one-posting edge comes from posting 11, which I relabeled after seeing the revision call it not relevant, so it is not an independent test.

What the specialty point did is clear even so. In the replies as received it changed exactly three verdicts, all from relevant to not relevant (postings 1, 4 and 11), and no other. On the ten postings that need judgment (the rule passes them), the revision matched my labels on 5 of 5 and 4 of 5, and the baseline and the rule on 3 of 5 each.

The rule (a record) scored 8 of 10 on both sets. Its held-out misses are posting 11 and posting 19, whose requirement "5+ years in one or more of: ML systems, performance engineering, distributed systems, or HPC." never says "experience", so check 7 as written skips it; one other rule-relevant posting on the board (Software Engineer, Data Infrastructure) has the same gap. Both prompts called posting 19 not relevant. The majority class scored 7 of 10 on both sets.

How little ten cases show: one posting moves a pass rate by 10 points, every interval overlaps the others, and each prompt answered each posting once, while the sampling report shows a reply can break the format. This experiment cannot say that either prompt is better.

## Notes on the evidence

- The validator as first written did not check the contract's limit of three sentences per justification. An independent review found the gap on 2026-10-30; the validator now enforces it, and every saved reply was re-checked, not asked again. Nine replies have four sentences: four in this experiment, five in the sampling.
- I relabeled posting 11 (Systems Software Engineer, Silicon Bringup) from relevant to not relevant on 2026-10-30, after the Claude calls, when the review pointed out that its required lines are the same kind of specialty I used for postings 1 and 4. The first label is kept in labels/labels.json and in commit 882909e.
- For the ten postings the rule rejects, I confirmed my labels in one answer, against the rule's record.
- Claude Code read the held-out postings' text and my held-out labels while preparing my labeling questions, before any Claude judge call, and then proposed the three candidate changes; I chose one from the development results. The held-out set ran once per prompt, after both development runs, as tools/experiment.py enforces.
- Before publishing, the command field of the saved calls was edited to write this Mac's home folder as ~, in every commit, so no account name is public. Nothing else in any record changed.
