Section 1: The Filter
My goal is to find software engineering and backend jobs that are a good match for Jane. I want the filter to consider her experience, location, work authorization and availability. I also want to avoid throwing away good opportunities just because a job title or description uses slightly different wording. Clear mismatches should be rejected, while less certain matches should stay in the results with an explanation.
The seven existing checks
R1 — Employment Type: KEEP (Hard)
Check the employment type field. Keep full-time positions and reject jobs that clearly state they are part-time, internships or temporary positions. Jane is looking for a full-time job. When the employment type is missing, mark it as unknown instead of assuming it is full-time or rejecting it.
R2 — Location: CHANGE (Soft)
Check the location field and any location details in the description. Jane prefers California, Austin, Boston, Cambridge, New York, Seattle, Bellevue, Redmond and US remote positions. If a job is outside these locations, lower its ranking and explain why instead of rejecting it immediately. I don't want to miss a potentially good opportunity just because the location is different. If the location is missing, mark it as unknown.
R3 — Job Level: CHANGE (Soft)
Check the job title for words such as Senior, Staff, Principal, Lead, Manager and Director. These titles can indicate a position that is above Jane's experience level. However, I don't want to reject a job based only on its title because companies sometimes use different titles for similar roles. Lower its ranking and explain the reason.
R4 — Job Role: KEEP (Hard)
Check the job title and role description to see whether the position involves software or backend engineering. Keep roles such as Software Engineer and Backend Engineer, including relevant platform and infrastructure engineering positions. Reject clearly unrelated roles such as Frontend Engineer, Mobile Engineer, Researcher and Security Engineer. Jane is mainly looking for backend and software engineering work, so I want this requirement to remain strict.
R5 — Work Authorization and Sponsorship: KEEP (Hard)
Check the job description for requirements involving US citizenship, security clearance or statements that the company cannot provide sponsorship. Reject a job when its requirements clearly conflict with Jane's situation. Jane is an F-1 student, is not a US person and will need H-1B sponsorship in the future. However, when a posting does not mention sponsorship, keep it rather than assuming that the company will not sponsor.
R6 — Start Date: CHANGE (Soft)
Check the description for the expected start date. Jane's earliest available full-time start date is May 2027. When a company expects someone to start before that, lower the ranking and explain the conflict rather than automatically rejecting the job. If the start date is not mentioned, do not treat it as a problem.
R7 — Years of Experience: CHANGE (Hard)
Check the job description for the minimum years of experience required. Jane has three years of full-time work experience, so reject positions that clearly require more than three years as a mandatory qualification. However, phrases such as "experience is a plus", "preferred" or "nice to have" should not count as mandatory requirements. Also, ignore numbers that are unrelated to job experience, such as a company saying it grew 10x in two years. I want the filter to understand what the sentence actually means instead of rejecting jobs based on numbers alone.
New topic words
I also want the filter to look for technical topics in the job description, not just the job title. These words should help improve a job's ranking, but mentioning a word alone should not make a posting relevant.

* Backend development: Look for actual backend software development work, not general office or back-office tasks.
* APIs: Match work involving building, maintaining or developing APIs, not just a passing mention of an API.
* Distributed systems: Match engineering work involving distributed systems, not companies mentioning that their employees work remotely across different locations.
* Databases and SQL: Match actual database development, management or engineering work, not a general mention of customer databases.
* Cloud infrastructure: Match building and maintaining cloud services and infrastructure, not simply using cloud storage.
* Microservices: Match designing and developing microservice-based applications, not unrelated mentions of services.
* Developer tools and platforms: Match building software tools and platforms for developers, not general business platforms.

These topics are useful because they help identify jobs that match Jane's backend engineering interests. They should be used as ranking signals rather than automatic requirements.
Additional rule
R8 — AI Training and Data Annotation: ADD (Hard)
Check the job description for positions where the main responsibility is labeling data, rating AI responses or performing basic AI training and annotation tasks. Reject these positions because they are not the type of software engineering work Jane is looking for. However, do not reject genuine engineering positions involving machine learning systems, model development, training pipelines or AI evaluation tools. I want to avoid unrelated AI gig jobs without accidentally removing real engineering opportunities.
