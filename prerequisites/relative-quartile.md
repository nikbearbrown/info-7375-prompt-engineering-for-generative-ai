# Relative Quartile — 20 points out of 100

**Course AI policy:** [AI Policy for Professor Bear's Courses | Using AI Responsibly in Class](https://youtu.be/8Ut0Cdl6vMw?si=9w3aEpt1ZyAR4Kiz).

For enrolled NEU students. Read with the [AI policy](ai-policy.md).

Assigned after all submissions for the same assignment are reviewed. This component reflects overall quality relative to peers: specificity, substantive improvement over the starting point, demonstrated understanding, professional communication, honesty about verification, and evidence of actually running or simulating the workflow rather than merely describing it.

| Quartile | Points | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| Top 25% | 16–20 | Among the strongest submissions: specific, thoughtfully refined, supported by clear evidence, candid about limitations, and professionally explained. |
| Second 25% | 10–15 | Strong work with demonstrated understanding and useful evidence, but less depth, refinement, completeness, or communication effectiveness than the strongest submissions. |
| Third 25% | 5–9 | Comparatively limited development: generic explanations, thin evidence, weak improvement over the starter, or communication that makes the work harder to evaluate. |
| Bottom 25% | 0–4 | Least developed relative to the cohort: substantial gaps in specificity, support, understanding, refinement, or usability. Placement alone does not imply misconduct. |

These are continuous 20-point bands, not a literal multiplication of the older five-point table. The instructor selects the score within each band based on the strength of the submission. A bottom-quartile placement is not an automatic zero.

## What reviewers compare

- **Specificity and contribution:** A concrete problem, meaningful decisions, credited starting material, and identifiable improvements—not generic AI-generated description.
- **Execution and evidence:** Inspectable outputs, tests, demonstrations, or clearly labeled simulations. Say what was run, what was checked, and what remains unverified. Never represent a simulation as a live run.
- **Understanding and judgment:** Ability to explain the mechanism, justify choices, identify limits, and respond to instructor or TA questions.
- **Refinement and professional communication:** An organized, readable, usable submission whose explanation helps another person understand and inspect the actual work. Visual polish supports substance; it cannot replace it.

These are comparative considerations, not four additional point buckets. Apply them within the assignment's scope: a simulation is appropriate where permitted, not a substitute for a required live implementation.

## Optional tools, shared quality expectations

An explainer video is itself optional and has no separate points. A submission without a video can earn full credit; compare how effectively the chosen format communicates the work, not whether a video exists.

Brutalist is one optional way to improve an explainer. If classmates use it effectively and your explanation is less clear or professional, that quality difference can affect your relative score. Using Brutalist does not itself improve your rank, and not using it does not itself lower it. A clear, accurate screen recording can earn the highest score. Tool spending, animation volume, and unnecessary extra features are not grading criteria.

## Review procedure

1. Review the full comparison group before assigning quartiles. Coordinate judgments across TAs using the same criteria and shared examples.
2. Compare demonstrated quality, not presumed effort, paid-tool access, commit count, or production style. Give a short rationale identifying strengths and the most important quality gap.
3. Distinguish this component from Frictional: Frictional documents honest effort and struggle; Relative Quartile compares the resulting work. An unresolved struggle can earn full Frictional credit without making the artifact top-quartile.

Before grading, publish how ties, uneven cohort sizes, late submissions, and resubmissions will be handled. Those administrative rules are not specified in the supplied transcript; see [instructor decisions](../docs/instructor-decisions.md). Do not silently break ties or claim exact quarters when the group size cannot support them.

The remaining 80 points are 60 implementation, 10 Frictional, and 10 proper GitHub version posting. Graded Assignments occur every 10 days; lesson Assessments are ungraded. Do not add these 20 points on top of an existing 100-point rubric; they replace its comparative-quality component.
