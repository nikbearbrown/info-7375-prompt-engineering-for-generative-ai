# NEU prerequisite: AI policy

**Course AI policy:** [AI Policy for Professor Bear's Courses | Using AI Responsibly in Class](https://youtu.be/8Ut0Cdl6vMw?si=9w3aEpt1ZyAR4Kiz).

For students taking INFO 7375 at Northeastern. Read before beginning graded work. This is course policy, not a numbered book chapter.

## Use AI to learn, then refine

AI assistance is allowed and expected. This course uses Claude and Python, with Claude Code access assumed. The purpose is to help you learn and produce stronger work—not to substitute a finished answer for your understanding. Iterate: examine the first result, identify weaknesses, revise, test, and improve it.

AI use by itself is not cheating. It also does not establish that your work is correct, that you understand it, or that it deserves a high grade.

## Cite your starting point and explain your contribution

Credit starter assignments, repositories, examples, collaborators, and AI assistance. Link the original source and identify what you changed or improved. Do not submit an unchanged previous assignment as your own contribution.

State what Claude contributed and what you personally attempted, checked, revised, and decided. Preserve an honest account of effort in your Frictional log; do not ask AI to invent your struggle or understanding.

## Be able to explain what you submit

The instructor or a TA may ask you to explain your code, writing, equations, methods, or results after submission. You remain responsible for understanding AI-assisted work. Inability to explain submitted work can lose points under the relevant assessment criteria; AI assistance alone is not an automatic failure.

Distinguish actual runs from simulations, fixtures, proposals, and unverified claims. Report limitations plainly. A professional explanation does not make unsupported results valid.

## Twenty points are relative to your peers

Graded Assignments are due every 10 days and worth 100 points: 60 for implementation, 10 for Frictional, 10 for proper GitHub version posting, and 20 for overall quality relative to classmates submitting the same assignment. Existing lesson Assessments and quizzes are ungraded practice. Meeting the stated requirements can earn the criterion-based 80 points; it does not guarantee the comparative 20.

The instructor and TAs review all submissions before assigning the [Relative Quartile score](relative-quartile.md). This is a qualitative professional judgment informed by evidence, not an automatic metric or a count of hours. Because everyone can use AI, a quick first draft may be substantially weaker than classmates' tested, refined, clearly explained work.

## Brutalist is optional; communication quality matters

You are **not required to use Brutalist** to make assignment explainers. Use another suitable recording or presentation workflow if you prefer. The explainer video itself is optional, not just the choice of tool. It may strengthen communication under Relative Quartile; it has no separate point allocation. A submission without a video can earn full credit.

If peers use Brutalist to produce clear, readable, evidence-rich explanations and yours is difficult to follow, your submission may compare less favorably in professionalism. The difference is the quality of the explanation—not the software name. A precise screen recording can outperform a polished animation that obscures the work. Brutalist use earns no automatic bonus; choosing another tool incurs no automatic penalty.

No paid media service or public YouTube upload is required by this policy. The [optional Brutalist guide](brutalist-video.md) offers one available workflow.
