# Irreducibly Human: companion readings

These closing notes adapt the main chapters of *Irreducibly Human*, reviewed on 2026-09-06, to INFO 7375. Their organizing question is explicit: **what should AI do, and what should the human do?** The answer is task-specific. Claude helps produce, inspect, calculate, and challenge; the learner develops understanding and owns the situated decisions, relationships, and permissions.

## Locating the chapters

The manuscript is not bundled with this repository. In the instructor's workspace, it is the sibling directory `../irreducibly-human-book/`, relative to the course root. The files below are in that directory's `chapters/` folder. Ask Claude Code to open the named passage if you have the checkout, or use the matching chapter in the instructor-supplied edition. Public readers can use the self-contained lesson notes without the book. No public book or film URL is assumed.

The reading pass covered the introduction, Chapters 1–7, and conclusion. The supplemental themes, research-paper drafts, and media-production files are not assigned here. The manuscript contains unresolved source-verification flags and philosophical arguments about permanent limits. These course notes do not reproduce unverified statistics or treat those arguments as established capability theorems. They prescribe responsibilities for these exercises, not an immutable ranking of minds.

## Foundations before offloading

Chapter 1, **Tier 1: Pattern & Association**.
File: `01-tier-1-pattern-and-association.md`.

Read “Beneficial offloading, detrimental offloading” and “The baseline-knowledge condition: you cannot audit what you do not know.” Week 1 preserves the first implementation and explanation as learning work, while using Claude for feedback and routine assistance afterward. This is the chapter's distinction between freeing capacity and surrendering the capability needed to evaluate the result. The note does not claim that all human review helps or that AI always outperforms people on pattern tasks.

## Prediction and intervention

Chapter 5, **Tier 5: Causal & Counterfactual**.
File: `05-tier-5-causal-and-counterfactual.md`.

Read “Pearl's ladder, and the rung the machine lives on” and “Synthesis and bridge.” Week 9 distinguishes a fitted classifier's response from an intervention's real-world effect. Claude may help with notation, hypotheses, calculations, and tests; the learner must justify the question, assumptions, and evidence. The chapter itself cautions that the information limit of observational data is not an architectural impossibility proof. Neither a human nor a model can extract an unidentified effect from insufficient information merely by asserting a causal story.

## Independence and deliberation

Chapter 6, **Tier 6: Collective & Distributed**.
File: `06-tier-6-collective-and-distributed.md`.

Read “What makes a group smart — and the friction the machine throws away,” “The applied companion,” and “Synthesis and bridge.” Week 12 distinguishes AI coordination from human deliberation, using independent initial judgments before a shared synthesis. Preserve dissent and local knowledge instead of inferring independence from different agent names. The chapter's “invisible groupthink” label is presented as a hypothesis; the note uses its sequencing practice without claiming the exercise establishes that hypothesis.

## Review capacity

Chapter 4, **Tier 4: Metacognitive & Supervisory**.
File: `04-tier-4-metacognitive-and-supervisory.md`.

Read “Plausibility auditing: the named skill no curriculum teaches,” especially the volume-trap discussion. Week 13 gives the human an actual review budget and the system a pending-review behavior. AI-assisted checks can reduce workload, but a model's confidence and a recorded fingerprint do not demonstrate that a person reviewed the proposal. The source's broader assertions about uncertainty and hallucination are not necessary to this operational lesson.

## Relationships and consent

Chapter 3, **Tier 3: Social & Personal**.
File: `03-tier-3-social-and-personal.md`.

Read “How social intelligence is actually built — and how it is unbuilt” and “The machine boundary.” Week 14 uses Claude to prepare and document communication while people conduct stakeholder consultation, obtain permission, and maintain recourse. Simulated stakeholder responses are rehearsal material, not testimony or consent. This note applies the book's protected relational core to team governance; it does not turn the class into counseling or make clinical claims.

## Purpose and accountability

Chapter 7, **Tier 7: Existential & Wisdom**, and Chapter 8, **Conclusion — The Ladder and the Self**.
Files: `07-tier-7-existential-and-wisdom.md` and `08-conclusion.md`.

Read Chapter 7's “Wisdom is not the top of the knowledge hierarchy,” “The machine boundary: reckoning is not judgment,” and “The temporal honesty”; then the conclusion's “Deprioritization is not deletion — at full stakes.” Week 15 distinguishes evidence assembly and option comparison from the accountable choice to ship, narrow, defer, or stop. The book explicitly frames its strongest claim about wisdom as contested. Students need to defend their actual decisions, not profess agreement with a philosophical impossibility claim.

## Selection boundary

Notes appear only in Weeks 1, 9, 12, 13, 14, and 15. Each contributes a different division of labor to an existing artifact. The remaining lessons already assign implementation, checks, boundaries, and review; a further note would mostly repeat those assignments.

Chapter 2's embodied and sensorimotor discussion was read but is not mapped onto a Python lesson merely by analogy: this course does not teach physical skill acquisition. A future physical-world capstone may justify that connection, but would need its own concrete context and supervision requirements.

## How to use the notes

The heading is always “Irreducibly Human,” placed last after any relevant Anthropics, Computational Skepticism, and Conducting AI sections. Each note names what AI should do, what the human should do, and how to record the split. “Human should” is substantive work, not a ceremonial signature; “AI should” includes useful assistance, not a token mention.

All exercises continue to assume Claude Code access through NEU, or the reader's own Claude Code-enabled account. These notes require no direct API calls, additional model provider, new grading scheme, or extra capstone. Learner judgments and reflections remain the learner's work.
