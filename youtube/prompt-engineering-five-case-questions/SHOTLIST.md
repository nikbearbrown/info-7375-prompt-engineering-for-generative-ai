# Shot list

## BIDEA
Hello. This is Liam, in for Bear. Prompt Engineering has five case-style questions worth fifty points. Each case asks five specific questions about the evidence. You do not write an open-ended essay. You answer those five questions directly.

## BDEFS
Four terms. A planted flaw: a deliberate mistake the exam author hid in the agent's work. The exact term: the precise name for that kind of mistake. An evidence request: one check anyone could run to settle a claim. And scope: what the request or the claim actually covers, and no more.

## B00
The full exam is worth one hundred points. Five cases contribute fifty points. Ten multiple-answer questions contribute the other fifty. The case section tests whether you can judge an agent’s work, not whether you can write a long essay.

## B01
Every case in Professor Bear's exams has the same four pieces. The first is a scenario: a situation with a stakeholder, who is whoever will rely on the work, and a goal they want met.

## B02
The second piece is the instruction: the short request that was sent to the agent, in plain words.

## B03
The third is the agent's output. It is confident and well written, and it contains at least one planted flaw. Planted means the exam author put it there on purpose, so there is at least one to find.

## B04
Every case prints five specific questions. First, identify a real defect and explain why. Second, name the precise concept. Third, propose an evidence check. Fourth, write a corrected instruction. Fifth, explain the consequence plainly. The wording points you to this case’s claim, evidence and decision-maker.

## B11
Now, how it is scored. Each case is worth ten points, in five rows. Finding a real defect, and justifying it, is worth three. Using the exact term, two. Asking for evidence, two. Giving a fix or next step, two. And keeping to scope, and saying it in plain language, one.

## B12
One rule sits over all of it. If you name no real defect, with a reason a grader can check, the case is capped at five out of ten, however good the rest sounds. The author's planted flaw is the one they expect, but you do not have to guess it. Any real defect counts, if you can show why.

## B13
Short numbered answers are enough. Answer the five questions that were actually asked. Grammar, polish and length earn nothing. Specific reasoning grounded in the case earns credit, including for writers working in a second language.

## B14
Now, how to approach one. Read the prompt contract: task, allowed tools, target, and required output. Compare that with what the agent actually did. A claim that it finished is not proof that it followed the contract.

## B16
Ask for observable proof. Run this. Show me the failing test. Open the log. Never just say, double-check it, because the agent can double-check and say the same thing again.

## B17
Give a fix precise enough to execute, a message the agent could act on with no more questions. And keep to scope, which means answering what the case asks, and saying no more than the evidence supports.

## BHTF
Your turn. In your Prompt Engineering practice project, ask: Give me one case with five specific questions. Wait for my answers. Check that you received five questions, not an essay prompt. Check that the points add to ten.
